⚠️ Why Transformer oil is to be tested?

Transformer oil testing is essential for ensuring the reliable operation and longevity of transformers. It serves multiple functions: electrical insulation, cooling, and arc suppression. Regular testing is necessary for:

  • Detecting Electrical Faults: Identify partial discharges and arcing promptly.
  • Assessing Oil Condition: Checking dielectric strength, moisture, and acidity.
  • Preventing Downtime: Proactive maintenance reduces risk of unexpected failures.
  • Ensuring Safety: Preventing fire, explosions, and environmental pollution.

📊Technical Data

DGA is the most important tool for detecting incipient faults in oil-filled electrical equipment. gases like H2, CO, CO2, CH4,C2H2, C2H4, C2H6, O2, N2 are analyzed.

Moisture content in transformer oil affects the dielectric properties and paper insulation life. It is measured in ppm (parts per million).

Measures the ability of the oil to withstand electric stress. High BDV indicates low moisture and contaminants.

IFT measures the surface tension between oil and water, indicating the presence of polar contaminants and oil decay products.

The lowest temperature at which the oil gives off enough vapor to ignite. Low flash point indicates contamination with volatile oils.

Testing for solid particles that can block cooling ducts or settle on windings.

Measures the dielectric dissipation factor. High values indicate contamination or deterioration of oil.

Specific resistance of the oil. High resistivity is indicative of low ionic contamination.

Specific gravity of the oil, important for oil circulation and heat dissipation.

Neutralization value indicating the amount of acidic components produced during oxidation.

Measures the resistance to flow. Crucial for the cooling properties of the transformer.
